# Q&A: Mubit's constraints, and why our numbers still hold

Short answers for judges, and the Mubit team in particular. The tone is constructive: what we measured, what we did about it, what would help every team. Never say "Mubit doesn't work". Every number carries its R-id from `results_log.md`; field-report items are `F#` in `mubit_field_report.md`.

**Positioning (D54):** Busy Machines is **compute intelligence for training sweeps**: the compute layer that decides when the AI planner is worth calling, keeps every machine busy and blocks fakes. Mubit is the cheap-AI layer next to it. Different layers, and they multiply.

**Spoken short form (15 s):** "Different layers. Mubit makes each AI call cheaper; Busy Machines decides how many calls a sweep needs and keeps the machines busy. None of Mubit's small models answers our calls yet: our best checking model matches Opus on 58.3% of its exam (R79; the same data in another workspace 11.4%, R70), and the bar is 95%, so Mubit keeps Opus answering, as designed. Our result is our layer alone, on Opus through Mubit: the same model as an Opus-driven sweep for a quarter of the AI bill on two tasks (R78, a tie; R82, within 0.0013 with Opus ahead in every run), with two logged limits (R75, planned too rarely; R85, long runs on real GPUs, 5% worse). The day a Mubit model passes, the two multiply."

## 1. Why no adapter serves
| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **The exam bar is high.** Real tasks show a **95%** match target on the adapter cards; the toy task showed 85%. Project B's Settings → Going live says the quality bar is **90%**. A move also needs a 0.50 floor and ≥20 test cases | R7, R28, R30, R46, R56 |
| **Scores so far, all below target:** planning 14.3% (the card said "Prompt only", which may mean the base model plus prompt, not a trained adapter; unconfirmed), checking v0 42.9% (6 of 14; the base model with the prompt 1 of 14), toy 70% vs 85% | R7, R28, R30 |
| **The closed-output checker scored lower in B, higher in C:** **11.4% on 35 test cases after ~7 h in training** (project B), although Opus agrees with itself 40 of 40 on that output; workspace C's copy, trained on the same inputs (159 kept), scored **58.3% on 24 test cases**. Same data, very different scores, which suggests test-set noise or a B-specific format problem; not verified | R39, R70, R79 |
| **Training was slow or stalled:** B's job showed "Training" for ~7 h before v1 appeared (at 11.4%); workspace C's (same data, same task key) trained ~3 h after it started. The toy task trained in ~5 min on Friday | R47, R55, R68, R70, R79, R6 |
| **Diagnose training failed** at first, with no reason given; Mubit retried it, and the new version scored 8.3% on 12 test cases vs 95% | R46, R80 |
| **No routes:** no task has a route, so nothing has gone live, silent test included | R28–R30, R46, R70 |
| **No way to pin an adapter yet.** Mubit support (Discord, 24 Oct 11:51): the auto-router decides; a header to name the adapter per request is coming "later". It had not shipped at the time of writing | `mubit_office_hours.md` (Discord answer) |
| **The planning step can't pass a match exam by nature:** many different experiment plans are equally good | R28, F11 |
| **Review and data limits shaped the first adapters:** ~200 reviews per task per day (our reading of the queue), so the first planning adapter trained on 29 of 499 calls | R29, F8 |
| Early stopping never got an adapter: Opus agrees with itself only 92.5–95% on it, below the 97% line we set, so we made it a rule and then switched it off (no rule passed our offline gate) | R31, R43, R57, D39, D43 |

## 2. What we did about it
| Action | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Changed our outputs to suit the exam:** the checker's free-text reason became a verdict plus a reason code from a fixed list. Opus self-agreement 68/68, Haiku vs Opus 65/68 zero-shot | D37, R32 |
| **A gate before recording any new task:** Opus must agree with itself ≥97%, ≥150 distinct inputs, no class >70%, no free text. Early stopping failed it, so we didn't spend a training on it | D39, R31 |
| **Fresh workspaces for fresh queues:** project **B** (judge v1, 177/177 recorded) and workspace **C** (identical hedge recording when B stalled; grouped in ~7 min vs ~1 h on B) | D38, R39, R59, R61 |
| **Go-live settings tuned on B:** silent test 20% of calls (up to 100 a day), partial rollout 50% (at least 100 calls); the quality bar left at 90% | R56 |
| **A go-live kit, ready and $0 until used:** `scripts/push_judge_traffic.py` (traffic for silent test and rollout, capped, never touches project A), `scripts/eval_served_calls.py` (grades the calls the console says the adapter answered, against ground truth **and** against Opus), 3 held-out Try-models cases with Opus's expected answers, and a runbook | `adapter_golive_runbook.md`, `data/adapter_golive/` |
| **v2 triggered:** 40 new Opus-labelled checks recorded into B, to reach "new versions after 25 new examples" | R58, R71 |
| **Adapter header ready:** `data/mubit_projects.json` takes an `adapter_headers` block. The client sends it only on the Mubit arm, and refuses it on any other arm | `mubit_office_hours.md`, `backend/agent/mubit_client.py` |
| **The product doesn't wait for Mubit:** the engine (lean planner, backfill, cadence, checker) runs on plain Opus through Mubit today, and each step switches to Mubit's model only when it passes (D36, D46) | D46 |
| **A field report for the Mubit team**, F1–F15 | `mubit_field_report.md` |

## 4. How our numbers stay valid
| Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Did every LLM call go through Mubit?" | Yes, a hard rule. Every race call carries a Mubit request ID (`mreq_…`) stored in SQLite and the JSONL export; the app never reads a provider key. Claude Code wrote code, never answered a race call (D15) | CLAUDE.md, D15 |
| "Could Mubit's router have answered some of the Opus arm's calls?" | Race calls run with `x-middleware-record: off` on project A. The docs say `off` drops the task fingerprint, so (by our reading, not yet confirmed by Mubit) those calls can't match a task or a route. Project A also has no route on any task. New tasks live in B and C, away from the baseline | D38, R46, R72, office hours §2 |
| "So 'Busy Machines' is Mubit?" | No, a different layer (D54): Busy Machines is the compute layer (when to call the planner, machines busy, fakes blocked); Mubit is the cheap-AI layer (each call cheaper). In every race so far, both sides are Opus 5.5 through Mubit. The difference is our compute layer, disclosed per arm in the race's fairness manifest. "Mubit + Busy Machines" is a separate arm, raced only once an adapter serves; `headline.py` refuses any Mubit framing without evidence that an adapter served | D41, D44, D46 |
| "Is the comparison fair?" | Equal and fixed: model, machines, slots, wall clock, $ cap, seeds, starting code, judge prompt. The standard arm is the plain v1 loop. The LCBench wide race was pre-registered (setup, seeds, success line) before any race, every seed is reported, and it failed (R75). The game-playing race's 60 s planner interval was chosen by an offline gate **before** that race (projected 0.22× LLM, 3.6% waiting; measured 0.25×, 1.5%), all 3 seeds reported (R78). The 30 s tabular follow-up was chosen **after** seeing R75, pre-registered before it ran, and is disclosed as such (D49); it met its line (R82) and is always reported beside R75 | D46, D47, D49, R75, R78, R82, `preregistration_lcbench_wide.md`, `preregistration_lcbench_wide_c30.md` |
| "Can you pool the game-playing seeds?" | Yes, after one rule fix: one diagnose call (made only after a failed run) split seed 2 off by fingerprint; D50 leaves that step out of the pooling key (it depends on whether a run failed, not on setup), and every earlier fingerprint id stayed unchanged | D50, R78 |
| "Are the no-AI tool's numbers in these races clean?" | Not fully. In races with a Busy Machines arm, Optuna shared one Python process with Busy Machines' sampler, which slowed its asks (~1.6×), so its waiting and experiment counts there are not isolated (its waiting may be inflated). Opus vs Busy Machines is essentially unaffected (~5 ms loop lag vs 4–13 s LLM calls). Fix planned after the races | R74 |
| "Can you pool races?" | Only with the same fairness fingerprint (arm set, features, prompt versions, record modes, settings). A race with a mid-race correction is blocked | D41 |
| "What does the LLM $ include?" | Token estimates at list price from the provider's usage fields, billed to our own Anthropic key. They exclude any Mubit fee (the org balance showed $50 with no visible run-fee deduction; race calls are record off, which keeps them outside learning tasks) | R72, D14 |
| "Is the GPU time real?" | The headline races did not use real GPUs, and we say so: game-playing runs on real Modal CPU containers (R78), and tabular tuning (LCBench) is a surrogate with simulated GPU time, run at 0.1× in the wide races (R82, disclosed). Image recognition runs on real L4s; our one Busy Machines race there failed its quality line (4.5× cheaper on AI, 5% more errors, R85). That is why we say "machines" or "compute", never "GPUs" alone (D54) | R38, R48, R53, R78, R82, R85, D47, D54 |
| "Did you cherry-pick?" | Every measurement is logged, worse ones included: R75 (our first pre-registered tabular race failed: 7% more errors; the follow-up R82 was chosen after it, and both are always reported together), R82 (even where it passed, Opus was ahead in every seed), R44 (our earlier engine made a model worse), R41 (a broken stopping rule), R67 (BM's AI bill doubled), R57 (Opus lost to a free rule on early stopping), R70 (11.4%), R77 (our Sapling v3 failed its gate), R85 (the real-GPU image race failed its quality line: 5% more errors) | `results_log.md` |
| "Is the Opus baseline handicapped?" | It gets the same caching, structured outputs and batching. The lean prompt is ours and is disclosed as a BM feature; its separate effect is quoted from its own test | R62, R63, D46 |

## 5. Hard questions, short answers
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "11.4% is worse than your free-text 42.9%. Why?" | "We don't know yet. A 3-verdict answer at 11.4% is near or below chance, which points to format or scoring, not learning, but that is a guess (R70). We haven't yet checked the adapter's own answers on its test cases; showing them beside the provider's would settle it (F15)." |
| "Why not lower the quality bar so it serves?" | "In the projects that matter, we didn't: the Opus baseline (A) and the Mubit arm's current route (B) keep their default bars (90% in B's settings, R56). In one separate workspace, C, we decided to lower it to 55% (D52), so its 58.3% checking model (R79) can try to go live. If it serves, we'll say 'we lowered the bar to 55%', never 'it passed Mubit's exam', and grade every answer it serves against ground truth, fakes it lets through included." Check the log for a go-live R-row before saying it served |
| "Isn't that what Mubit does?" | "Different layer. Mubit makes each AI call cheaper. We decide how many calls a sweep needs, and keep the machines busy in between. They multiply. Our 4× is our layer alone, on plain Opus through Mubit, measured before any Mubit model served (R78, R82; no adapter has answered a call, R70, R79). When Mubit's models pass, each of our fewer calls gets cheaper on top." Never "Mubit saved us 4×" |
| "Then what is Mubit adding today?" | "Every call through one gateway, with a request ID we store next to each experiment; the calls we record (race calls are record off, R72) become tasks and examples; the training pipeline; and a router that by Mubit's stated design switches only when its model is better (a launch claim we haven't measured), which is why our result doesn't depend on a model passing. When one does, it should serve without a code change on our side." |
| "Will the planning step ever be served?" | "Not with a match exam: many plans are equally good (R28). It's the slowest and most frequent step (13.1 s for 8 proposals, R48), so outcome grading would be the biggest win (F11)." |
| "Your correction didn't change a model, did it?" | "No. It is stored against the request ID and rated Bad in Mubit. There's no corrections API, and we don't know whether a rating affects training (Q2, Q9). What does change the model is new examples: 25 trigger a new version (R58), and we sent 40 (R71)." |
| "Is Haiku your small model?" | "No. Haiku was a speed test of a smaller provider model in the same loop (R50). It's not Mubit's model and not Busy Machines." |
| "Why did the tabular race fail?" | "We fixed the line before the race and missed it: 8.2× lower AI bill, but 7% more errors in all 3 runs (R75). Measured: the 90-second pace left 80 AI proposals per race, and there the AI planner found the shipped model in 3 of 3 runs, so it had too few ideas; and the standard assistant waited only 29.4%, not the 45.8% our offline gate projected (R66, flagged fragile; the success line was ≥40%), so there was less idle time to fill. So we ran one follow-up, a 30-second pace, chosen after the fail and pre-registered before it ran: 240 AI proposals per race, and it met its line, within 0.0013 (0.8884 vs 0.8897) for 0.25× the AI bill ($0.698 vs $2.774), though Opus was still ahead in every run (R82)." GPU $ there is virtual |
| "Did you pick the setting after seeing results?" | "For the tabular follow-up, yes, and we say so. R82's 30-second pace was chosen after R75 failed. We froze its setup and success line before it ran (`preregistration_lcbench_wide_c30.md`), declared it the last pace we'd try (D49), and report R75 beside it every time. It met the line on 3-run means; run 3 alone sat at the margin (−0.0030 vs the −0.003 line). The game-playing pace was set by an offline gate before its race (R78)." |
| "Is it just Optuna doing the work?" | "Partly, and here's what we measured. Game-playing (R78): the tuner inside Busy Machines ran ~1,466 of 1,587 experiments and found the shipped model in all 3 runs (R84). Tabular (R75, R82): the AI found it in 3 of 3 in both races; image recognition (R85): all of Busy Machines' bests came from the AI. In all four, Busy Machines beat the plain no-AI tool on quality (−154.3 vs −157.3; 0.8816 vs 0.8493; 0.8884 vs 0.8576; 0.9424 vs 0.9266, R74 caveat), and no fake got past its checker, where the unchecked tool accepted 53 as its best and shipped a fake in 3 of 3 runs (R78, R84), shipped one in 1 of 3 runs in each tabular race (R75 per R84, R82) and in 3 of 3 image runs (R85). What we haven't measured: a 'tuner plus checker, no AI' arm, so 'the AI picks the regions, the tuner fills the machines, the checker blocks the fakes' is the design, not a measured split. And in an earlier race without our arm the plain tool alone reached −155.2 on the same game-playing setup (R48, not pooled), close to ours." |
| "Where does the 4× come from? Mubit?" | "No, our compute layer on plain Opus, before any Mubit model served: planning calls at a steady pace (once a minute on game-playing, every 30 s on tabular), shorter plans, the tuner in between. On game-playing, in-race GPU money was the same on both sides (~$0.35, by arithmetic: total minus AI $); the AI bill was $0.83 vs $3.30 (list-price estimate), with a tied model, not a better one (R78). On tabular, $0.698 vs $2.774, within 0.0013 but Opus ahead in every run (R82, GPU $ virtual); our first tabular race, with a 90 s pace, was 8.2× cheaper on AI but failed on quality, 7% more errors (R75), and R82's pace was chosen after it. The checker's calls aren't paced yet: one run's burst of 143 checks cost $0.56. And on long image runs on real L4 GPUs the same layer was 4.5× cheaper on AI but 5% worse, failing its quality line (R85). No Mubit model has answered a call (R70)." |
