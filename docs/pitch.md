# Pitch: who we sell to, what we say, how we answer

**Stage rule (D19):** everything said on stage and shown on screen must make sense to someone who has never trained a model. Technical depth is for Q&A, the results export and the docs. Dashboard copy and demo narration are in `demo.md`.

## 0. Positioning (D54, governs every line below)
> ### Busy Machines: compute intelligence for training sweeps

- **What it is:** the compute layer for **AI-planned hyperparameter and RL training sweeps on a pool of machines (GPUs or CPUs)**. It does three things: it **decides when the AI planner is worth calling**, it **keeps every machine busy** in between with a no-AI sampler, and it **blocks fake improvements** before they ship.
- **Where Mubit fits:** Mubit is the **cheap-AI layer** next to it: it makes each AI call cheaper. Busy Machines decides **how many calls a sweep needs** and keeps the machines busy. The two multiply. **Our measured 4× came from Busy Machines alone, before any Mubit model served a call** (R70, R79, R80).
- **Customer:** ML and RL teams at AI labs that run sweeps (D18).
- **Not the category:** "AI research agent", "research assistant", "AI scientist", "intelligence for researchers" (crowded, and not what we measured). The AI planner (Opus 5.5 today, or a lab's own research agent) is the thing we sit under, not what we sell.
- **Headline (measured, R78, R82):** **"Same model as an Opus-driven sweep, a quarter of the AI bill, machines busy ~98%, no fakes shipped."** Game-playing: a tie, AI bill 0.25×, machines waiting 1.5% (R78). Tabular: within 0.0013 with the Opus sweep ahead in every run, AI bill 0.25×, machines waiting 1.1% (R82). Fakes shipped: 0 in both (0 of 847 and 0 of 85 trap runs accepted).
- **Always with the limits:** **plan too rarely and the model gets worse** (R75: planner every 90 s, AI bill 8.2× lower, 7% more errors, failed; R82's 30 s pace was chosen after it). **Long runs on real GPUs: cheaper, but a worse model** (R85: image recognition on real L4 GPUs, AI bill 0.22×, 5% more errors, 2.1× slower to the goal, quality line failed).
- **R84 corrections apply:** in R78 the shipped model came from the sampler in **3 of 3** runs; in R75 the no-AI tool shipped a fake in 1 of 3 runs; in R82 the Opus sweep accepted 0 of 70 trap runs.
- **Compute words:** say "compute" or "machines (GPUs or CPUs)", **never "GPUs" alone**, and never imply the best results ran on GPUs: R78 ran on cloud CPU workers, R82 on simulated GPU time, and the only Busy Machines race on real GPUs (R85, L4) failed its quality line.

### Why contamination is the right trap
- It's a **real, current AI-lab problem**: benchmark contamination and test-set leakage affect papers, leaderboards and launch decisions.
- It's **the verifier-bias story Anil wrote about**: an agent judging its own result with shared context approves fake gains; an independent verifier doesn't.
- It gives **ground truth** (we plant it), so the correction moment is measurable, not a matter of opinion.

## 2. The message
### The headline (D54, measured)
> ### Same model as an Opus-driven sweep · a quarter of the AI bill · machines busy ~98% · no fakes shipped

R78 and R82, always with the limits (R75, R85) beside it (§0). The three promises below are what Busy Machines is built to do; the headline is what we measured.

### The three promises (built to do; only the measured parts are claims)
> ### 🏆 Get a better model · 💸 Spend less · ⚡ Train faster

Order per D48: a better model and less money lead; machines waiting is *why it works*, not a promise of its own. "Spend less" means the **total** bill (compute + the planner's own AI calls): on short runs the AI calls are most of it (90.6% on the game-playing task, R54), and the compute $ lost to waiting is small next to them (R54), so never sell it as "spend less on GPUs".

Every slide, screen and sentence supports one of these. If it doesn't, cut it. Each is tied to a measured metric, and we never claim one we didn't measure:

| Promise | Metric that proves it |
|---|---|
| 🏆 Better model | best held-out score at the standard assistant's spend (same money) |
| 💸 Less money | total $ (compute + AI) to reach the standard assistant's quality (same quality); quality per $ |
| ⚡ Hit the target faster | time-to-target |
| Why it works (not a promise) | machines waiting % |

### Why "better model" follows from "faster and cheaper"
> **"Because the machines wait less, more of your budget goes into actual training, and more training means a better model."**

Fewer, better-timed planner calls → more experiments on the same machines → more chances to find better settings → a better final model. This is the chain we expect; the race **measures** it (same budget, same time, compare quality). **What the races show so far (Busy Machines vs the standard Opus-driven sweep):**
- **Game-playing AI (an RL sweep on 30 cloud CPU workers), 3 runs, headline-eligible (R78):** the chain held for cost, not for quality. Machines waiting 1.5% vs 68.8%, 10× the experiments per $, and **the same model (a tie, −154.3 vs −154.5) for 4.0× less AI money** ($0.83 vs $3.30 per race) and 3.1× less in total ($1.18 vs $3.65). Not a better model.
- **Tabular tuning, pre-registered follow-up, 3 runs, headline-eligible (R82, D49): success line MET (4 of 4).** Practically **the same model: 0.8884 ± 0.0017 vs 0.8897 ± 0.0013**, within 0.0013 (about 1% more errors), but **the standard agent was ahead in every run** (−0.0001, −0.0008, −0.0030). **AI bill 0.25×** ($0.698 vs $2.774 per race, 4.0× less) and 35% less in total ($3.89 vs $5.97, GPU $ virtual). Never reached the standard agent's final best (0/3), and to 0.86 it was slower and dearer ($1.14 / 4.2 min vs $1.08 / 2.9 min). Its planner pace (every ≥30 s) was **chosen after R75 failed** and pre-registered before the race ran.
- **Tabular tuning, the first pre-registered race, 3 runs (R75): FAILED its success line.** Planner every ≥90 s: AI bill 8.2× lower, but **7% more errors** (0.8816 vs 0.8892) in every run. Always said beside R82.
- **Image recognition on real L4 GPUs, 3 runs, planner every ≥60 s (R85): quality line FAILED.** AI bill 0.22× ($0.233 vs $1.056 per race, 4.5× less) and total 2.0× less ($0.83 vs $1.66), machines waiting 0.0% vs 12.0%, 0 of 12 trap runs accepted, but **5% more errors** (0.9424 vs 0.9451, behind in every run), never reached the Opus sweep's final best (0/3), and to the 0.94 goal it was 16% dearer and 2.1× slower; its shipped model (0.9395) was below the goal. On long runs the machines barely wait (12%), the sampler's runs are long and weak, so fewer AI proposals cost quality. This is our only Busy Machines race on real GPUs.
- **Without a pace limit (R67, 2 runs):** a better model (−153.9 vs −156.2), but 2.1× the AI bill.

So on stage: "same model as an Opus-driven sweep, a quarter of the AI bill, machines busy ~98%, no fakes shipped" on two tasks (R78 a tie, R82 within 0.0013 with the Opus sweep ahead in every run), always with the limits beside it: R75 (planned too rarely, 7% worse; R82's setting was chosen after it) and R85 (long runs on real GPUs, 5% worse). "A better model" stays a promise until a logged race shows it without a higher bill.

We expect better quality for two reasons, and the race measures both: **more experiments in the same budget**, and **verified results** (the checker rejects fake gains, so the "better" number is real). Note that more experiments aren't the whole story: on LCBench the Opus agent beat Optuna on quality while running 0.3× the experiments per dollar (R38, repeated in R44), and on image recognition at 0.5× (R53).

### One curve, two wins
- **Fix the budget → better quality** (best held-out score at the same budget).
- **Fix the quality target → less money** ($-to-target).

> **"A better model for the same money, or the same model for less money. You choose."**

This answers Anil's objection ("you're not saving money, you'll burn those tokens on something else"). We agree: the saved money **becomes more experiments, which becomes better quality.**

### Scope (say it precisely; it makes the claim defensible)
One narrow use case (D54): **AI-planned hyperparameter and RL training sweeps on a compute pool** (GPUs or CPUs). We measured it on short runs (15–90 s): an RL sweep on cloud CPU workers (R78) and a tabular sweep with simulated GPU time (R82). On long runs on real GPUs it did **not** hold on quality (R85). We do **not** claim to speed up a single giant pretraining run, and we are not an AI research agent: we sit under one.

## 5. The commercial story: two layers that multiply (D13, reframed by D54)
Busy Machines is the **compute layer** for AI-planned training sweeps; Mubit is the **cheap-AI layer** next to it. Mubit makes each AI call cheaper; Busy Machines decides how many calls a sweep needs, keeps every machine busy and blocks fakes. Sold together, the savings multiply, and every call Busy Machines makes already runs through Mubit (map in `architecture.md`). It is still a product Mubit could take to AI labs during launch week (the D13 "Mubit for Research" idea), but as a partner layer, not as a research agent.
